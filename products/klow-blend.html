Research overview

This vial combines GHK-Cu, BPC-157, TB-500, and KPV (50mg GHK-Cu + 10mg BPC-157 + 10mg TB-500 + 10mg KPV per vial (80mg total)).

GHK-Cu

GHK-Cu is a naturally occurring copper complex of the tripeptide glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine, first isolated from human plasma and also found in saliva and urine. It has been studied for roles in wound healing, collagen synthesis, attraction of immune cells, and antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity.

Also known asCopper tripeptide-1; glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine copper complex
Compound typeTripeptide–copper(II) complex

Molecular formulaC14H22CuN6O4 (complex); C14H24N6O4 (GHK)
Molecular weight340.4 (GHK peptide) g/mol
CAS number89030-95-5 (Cu complex); 49557-75-7 (GHK)

BPC-157

BPC-157 is a synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide derived from a protein found in human gastric juice. It has been studied mainly in laboratory animals, in models of tendon, muscle, and gastrointestinal injury. Human data are very limited, it is not approved by any drug regulatory agency, and the World Anti-Doping Agency has prohibited it since 2022.

TB-500

TB-500 is a synthetic, N-acetylated heptapeptide matching residues 17–23 of thymosin β4, the actin-binding region of that protein. In laboratory studies it inhibited the proliferation and migration of stimulated hepatic stellate cells, and topical application shortened wound-closure times in mice. It has not undergone human trials, and the World Anti-Doping Agency prohibits it.

Also known asThymosin β4 fragment 17–23

KPV

KPV is a tripeptide derived from the C-terminal fragment of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH). It has anti-inflammatory effects and has been researched in intestinal inflammation models, including work on its uptake by the PepT1 transporter.
